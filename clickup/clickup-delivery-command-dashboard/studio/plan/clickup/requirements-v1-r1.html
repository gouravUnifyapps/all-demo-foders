ClickUp Delivery Command dashboard: requirements

Client: Clickup · Use case: ClickUp Delivery command dashboard · Version 1, round 1 (kickoff) · Harvested 26 Oct 2026 · POC lead and manager: Gourav Mahunta

1. Summary

What the client wantsDelivery Command: one application where ClickUp's enterprise implementation team plans, staffs and reports on every customer implementation, customers follow and sign off their own rollout, and an AI assistant (Delivery Copilot) writes the routine status work. Seven stages per project: Kickoff, Discovery, Configuration, Migration, Training, Go-live, Hypercare.
Asks added104 in all: 50 P0, 53 P1, 1 for another use case
Why so many P0The BRD says every requirement in its § 5 is a Must for the first release unless marked Should; those, and lines worded "must", are P0 (open question 2).
Audience in the BRDImplementation lead, delivery director, resource manager, implementation consultant, customer project sponsor, VP of customer success (six personas, BRD § 4).
Sample data givenSix invented customers: Halden Freight (Logistics, Europe, 4,800 seats, Migration), Corvia Health (Healthcare, North America, 12,000, Configuration), Brightmoor University (Education, Europe, 2,300, Training), Kestrel Energy (Energy, North America, 7,500, Discovery), Sakura Mobility (Automotive, Japan, 9,200, Go-live), Ostrander Retail (Retail, North America, 650, Hypercare). The BRD states its team, customers, figures and rules are invented.
Not givenNo brand colour, logo, typeface file or design system: the BRD says to take them from clickup.com (A6, A7, A9).

3. Open questions

For the POC lead. Answer by number and letter, for example "1a, 2a, 3b". Option a is the recommended one each time.

  1. Scope of this use case. The POC is named "Delivery command dashboard", but the BRD (A1–A104) describes the whole Delivery Command application: 13 screen groups, an internal workspace, a customer portal, a phone view and an AI assistant. The BRD also calls itself an illustrative scenario.

    1. One app covering every ask, with the storyline led by the portfolio dashboard, the executive summary and Delivery Copilot (recommended)
    2. The dashboard screens only (portfolio, executive summary, Copilot); the other areas logged as other use cases
    3. The whole BRD with equal weight on every screen

  2. Priority. The BRD marks every § 5 requirement as Must unless marked Should, so 50 asks are P0 (all of § 5 except R5, plus the must-worded brand, navigation, sign-in and out-of-scope lines). Keep them all P0?

    1. Keep all 50 as P0; the plan shows each one somewhere, the storyline picks the lean-forward few (recommended)
    2. The lead names the P0 set for the 20-minute demo and the rest become P1

  3. A99, delivery director: "I want to see every project on one screen without scrolling." The BRD also says 180 to 300 projects and a paged list (A62, P1).

    1. A compact portfolio overview (health counts, risk grid, go-live timeline) above the paged list, so the whole portfolio reads on one screen (recommended)
    2. Every project as a dense tile wall on one screen
    3. Paged list only; the note is declined and recorded

  4. A100, consultants: "Let us edit the timeline straight from the phone."

    1. Decline: the phone keeps M1 and M2 (status, time, notifications); the timeline stays a desktop screen (recommended)
    2. Allow date changes from the phone with the W3 preview of the new go-live date

  5. A101 vs A102: the VP of customer success wants customers to see their health score; implementation leads say customers should never see internal health.

    1. Hide internal health from the portal; customers see stage, milestones and open actions (C1) only (recommended)
    2. Show a customer-facing readiness score in the portal, separate from internal health
    3. Show internal health in the portal

  6. A103, resource manager: "Colour-code consultants by region everywhere." The BRD also says meaning never depends on colour alone (A90) and keeps status colours (A70–A73).

    1. A labelled region tag with its own colour on consultant rows and avatars, never colour alone (recommended)
    2. Region colour in the resourcing grid only
    3. Decline and record it

  7. A104, Sales: "Add a pricing calculator for upsells." Logged under Other use cases; the BRD's out of scope (A96) excludes billing and revenue.

    1. Leave it out of this use case (recommended)
    2. Reply promote A104 to bring it in

  8. Demo data volume. The profile says fictional data, 15 to 20 rows; the BRD speaks of 180 to 300 projects, about 72 consultants and about 400 risks, and names six sample customers.

    1. 15 to 20 projects built around the six named sample customers, with headline counts on the dashboards seeded to read like the BRD's portfolio (recommended)
    2. Seed the full BRD volumes (about 250 projects, 72 consultants, 400 risks)

  9. Phone view (M1, M2, A61). The BRD asks for a phone with bottom tabs Today, Tasks, Time, Alerts.

    1. A responsive phone layout of the same code app, shown in a phone frame (recommended)
    2. A separate native mobile app

  10. Integrations (A92–A95, D3): Salesforce, Slack, Google Drive and Jira. The profile allows only connections already on the tenant.

    1. Use a connection where one already exists on the tenant; show the rest on the integration status screen with seeded sync times and one seeded Sync failed row (recommended)
    2. Seeded status only, no live connection at all

  11. Weekly executive email (E4) and the weekly Slack status post (A93).

    1. Generate the email and post as previews in the app, and send one real email to the presenter during the demo (recommended)
    2. Preview only, nothing sent
    3. Send to a demo Slack channel and inbox on a schedule
