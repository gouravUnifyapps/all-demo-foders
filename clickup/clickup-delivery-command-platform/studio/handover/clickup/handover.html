Scope

Delivery Command makes every ClickUp customer implementation visible and actionable in one place: portfolio health, risks and go-lives for the delivery director, the project plan, resourcing and timesheets for leads and resource managers, and change requests that route themselves to the right approvers. Customer sponsors get their own portal, in English, German or Japanese, to sign off deliverables, book training and upload user lists. Delivery Copilot, an AI assistant on every internal screen, answers delivery questions from the live data, draws charts and drafts the weekly status report, and labels everything it writes as AI-written.

Features

Portfolio at a glance (A9–A14)

Portfolio health, the risk grid and the on-track trend on one screen; the week-on-week changes read ±0 until the fix lands.

Inside a project (A15–A21)

Sakura Mobility's overview: days to go-live, budget hours used, open risks, the next sign-off and the seven-stage path.

Staffing without overbooking (A22–A26)

Every consultant against the next 12 weeks, with Haruto Sato's 110% week flagged with an icon and a word.

Change requests that route themselves (A27–A30)

A change of 40 hours or more goes to the delivery director, then the finance partner, and a rejection carries its written reason.

The Monday summary (A45–A48)

The six headline numbers with their weekly change and the contract value bridge from last month to this month.

The sponsor's portal (A39–A44)

Hana Mori sees only Sakura Mobility, in Japanese: milestones, upcoming sessions and the open actions for her team.

Delivery Copilot (A31–A38)

A plain-language question answered from live data, labelled AI-written, with links to the projects it names.
