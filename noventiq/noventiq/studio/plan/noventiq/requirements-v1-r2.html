Context: Noventiq’s business model (for A49)

Research context from public sources and the client’s deck, not asks. The G2 plan turns it into the persona and the AI features.

TopicWhat the sources say
What Noventiq isA global IT solutions and services provider (formerly Softline): software and hardware provisioning, public and private cloud, and associated services; one of Microsoft’s leading partners for software licensing and cloud. Focused on emerging markets in Eastern Europe, the Americas and Asia. Revenue about USD 1.6 billion (FY23, per Wikipedia, not checked against a filing).
How it earnsResale margin on software licences and cloud subscriptions, vendor funding and rebates (A13: “key income source”), and services (integration, cybersecurity, ERP, HR systems). Embee, its Indian business, was about 60% cloud revenue when acquired (2021 press release).
How it grewBy acquisition: Embee (India, majority 2021, full 2023), Squalio (Europe) and others. Each kept its own CRM, ERP, payroll and time tools (deck p.9), which is why customers, stages and services do not line up (A29, A23 to A25).
Likely central personaFinance in the group and country entities, who must sign off gross profit, rebates and reconciliation and do not trust AI without citations (A57); with country sales and account leads, who need one account view and the next best offer per customer (A1, A53). Open question 2 confirms.
Pain points the sources nameThree CRMs with different stage meanings (A29); no common customer key (A23); local service codes (A25); gross profit not tying to the accounts (A34); rebates booked late (A15); missing timesheets hiding overruns (A11, A15); people cost that must stay private (A38, A39); exceptions that only the partner can fix today (A36).
